## WHAT THIS FORMAT IS

A single piece of B-roll footage (dark coding / entrepreneur desk aesthetic), looped, with a short on-screen hook and a "read the caption" pointer. The entire story and payoff live in the written Instagram caption underneath the video, not in the video itself. This is NOT a multi-beat animated carousel-to-reel — there is no chart, no screen mock, no numbered body slides. Just one hook and one caption per reel.

## THE FLAGSHIP ANGLE

Lead most reels with some variant of: "I gave my outreach / ad account / business to Claude (or AI) and it did this." Rotate the specific mechanism and result each time — never repeat the same claim, number, or scenario as a prior reel. Occasional non-AI variants are fine (a build-log or results/time-lapse angle) as long as they still funnel toward the same underlying pitch: Ryan runs an automated system that finds and lands clients, and he'll run the same thing on the reader's business for free if they comment the keyword.

## THE GOAL

Not follower vanity metrics. Every reel exists to generate leads and clients for Ryan's marketing agency. The CTA is always a lead magnet (free audit/check/DM), never just a follow ask.

## FABRICATION STANDARD (THIS FORMAT ONLY — do not apply this loosened rule anywhere else)

Do not over-niche: never name Ireland or a specific vertical (no "physio clinics," no named towns). Generalize to "local businesses" / "my area." Numbers do not need to be strictly factual — they need to be BIG and worth reading, not modest and "realistic-sounding" in the boring sense. This is a deliberate, approved exception for this format only. But "big" has a ceiling now (see SCALE CALIBRATION — tightened again on 2026-08-18): the numbers still have to be plausible for what Ryan's agency actually does and actually charges. Go big on percentages, multiples, and aggregate totals. Do not go big by inventing a single-client contract value his agency would never realistically land.

## SCALE CALIBRATION (READ BEFORE WRITING A SINGLE HOOK)

His words: "keep the numbers realistic to our business, our pricing models etc, we are never signing a €124k client... but we could do €10k off of X amount of clients instead." Ryan runs Google Ads / Meta Ads / email marketing for small, local businesses — modest ad budgets, modest retainers. A single client signing a contract anywhere near six figures is not believable for this business and undermines the whole reel the moment a sharp reader does the math. Fix: keep single-client dollar claims small and real-world-plausible; get the "big" feeling from percentages, multiples, and AGGREGATE totals across several clients instead.

Concretely, four number types to rotate — use the right one for the right claim:
- **Percentages and multiples (the safest lever — use often).** These read as huge regardless of the underlying dollar amount, because they're relative: "150% increase in conversions," "cut CPA by 86%," "4x'd their bookings," "revenue up 380%." No upper ceiling problem here — a percentage doesn't imply a contract size, so this is where "impressive" and "believable" overlap best.
- **Cost/efficiency figures, kept small-business-scale.** CPA, ad spend, cost-per-lead figures should stay in the tens-to-low-thousands range a small local business would actually be spending — "CPA from €89 to €12," "cut monthly spend from €5k to €1.8k." Never inflate these into figures a corner shop or local clinic wouldn't recognize as their own numbers.
- **Single-client revenue results, capped realistic.** When a hook or caption attributes a revenue figure to ONE client, cap it in the roughly €1.5k–€15k range — a genuinely good result for a small local business (a strong month, a good quarter, a lead-gen win), not a contract value. Never claim a single client "signed," "closed," or "landed" a deal above that range — Ryan's agency does not sign clients at that scale, so that specific claim shape is banned regardless of number.
- **Aggregate totals across multiple clients — this is the new BIG lever.** To get a headline-sized number without an unrealistic single-client claim, roll it up across several clients or a time window instead: "€10k in new business across 4 clients this month," "generated €22k in new pipeline across 6 local businesses this quarter," "helped 5 clients cut ad spend by €14k combined." This is now the preferred way to hit a big, scroll-stopping figure — spread it across clients/time rather than pretending one small business generated it alone.
- Client-count growth claims should stay modest and plausible for a small agency: "2 clients to 6," "3 to 9 in one quarter" — not "2 to 40," which reads as a different scale of business entirely.
- The hook's whole job is still to make the reader think "how did they DO that" — not "that's a nice small win." Get there through percentage/multiple framing or aggregate totals, never by inflating a single small-business client's numbers past what's believable.

### THE HOOK FORMULA (use this as the default shape for every hook)

**"I [did X] and [it doubled/tripled/cut/found — a real figure] — [varied closer]."**

Ryan's own example, verbatim, is the original calibration target for the X-and-Y spine: "I gave my Google Ads to Claude and saw a 150% increase in conversions, here's how." That "did X AND saw Y" backbone is still correct and non-negotiable. What changed on 2026-08-18 (second correction) is the closer at the end — Ryan flagged that always ending on "here's how" reads weak and doesn't fit every reel. Two fixes now apply together:

**Fix 1 — the "and" clause always carries a real number.** Never let the result half of the sentence go vague. "I gave my Meta Ads to Claude for 30 days" is a fine setup on its own, but it is NOT a complete hook until it's followed by "...and it tripled conversions" / "...and CPA dropped 78%" / "...and it doubled my leads." The word "and" is doing real work here — it's the hinge between action and result, so treat a hook that's missing it (or that trails off without a figure) as incomplete. If the exact number is less important than the shape of the win, a multiplier word is a fully acceptable substitute for a percentage — "doubled," "tripled," "4x'd," "cut in half" all count as the required figure.

**Fix 2 — rotate the closer instead of defaulting to "here's how" every time.** Research into how real results-driven marketing/business accounts on Instagram actually write this (both competitor accounts and copywriting-formula research) shows "here's how" is one option among many, and leaning on it every single time is what made it feel weak. Real accounts vary the promise based on what the reel is actually about — an audit reveals a process, a mistake reveals a fix, a comparison reveals a breakdown. Rotate across these closer types, never repeating the same one twice in a batch:
- **Process-reveal:** "Here's exactly how." / "Here's exactly what I changed." / "Here's the process."
- **Story/result reveal:** "Here's what happened." / "Here's what I found."
- **Steal-it framing:** "Steal the process below." / "Copy the exact process below."
- **Caption-pointer framing:** "Full breakdown below." / "The playbook's in the caption."
- **Reverse-engineer framing:** "Reverse-engineering it below." / "Breaking down exactly how below."
- Occasionally, when the number itself is the whole story, the hook can end on the number with no closer phrase at all and just a period — the caption pointer ("full story below," "read the caption") still needs to exist somewhere in the post, but it doesn't have to be the last words of the hook every time. Use this sparingly (at most 1 in a batch) so the hook doesn't feel unfinished.

Structure every hook the same way:
1. **X = the action** — what was handed over or done ("I gave my [ad account / cold email / CRM] to Claude," "I let AI run my [outreach]," "I handed a client's [ads] to a bot"). This can and should lead the hook — it's the setup, not throat-clearing.
2. **Y = the result, always a real figure or multiplier** — sized per SCALE CALIBRATION above: prefer a percentage or multiple (uncapped, always safe — "tripled," "cut CPA by 78%," "4x'd bookings"), or an aggregate figure across several clients/a time window if it's a dollar amount. If Y is a SINGLE client's revenue, keep it in the realistic €1.5k–€15k range — never a contract value bigger than a small local business would plausibly sign. Never let Y be vague ("saw amazing results," "it worked") — it must always land on a specific number or a doubled/tripled-style multiplier.
3. **The closer — rotate per the list above** — end the hook with a promise that the mechanism/story is in the caption. Vary which type of closer you use based on what actually fits this specific reel's angle (an audit reel earns "here's what I found," a process reel earns "here's the process," etc.) rather than defaulting to the same phrase every time.
- Full sentences are correct here, not fragments — write it the way you'd say it out loud as one clear statement, then break it across lines wherever it naturally pauses.
- Every hook must clear the SCALE CALIBRATION bar above for the size AND plausibility of Y — impressive, but a number Ryan's actual small-business clients could recognize as something in their own ballpark, never a small vanity metric (reply counts, DM counts) and never an inflated single-client contract value.
- No more than ~16 words total across all lines (slightly longer than before since this format is a full sentence, not fragments — but still tight, no filler words).
- Never reuse the exact X (what was handed over), the exact Y figure, or the exact closer phrase from a hook you generated earlier in the same batch.
- Rotate the X across a batch: ad account, cold email sequence, CRM/lead list, outreach DMs, landing page, client onboarding — never the same mechanism twice in one batch.
- Rotate the Y across a batch: percentage increase, cost/efficiency figure, single-client revenue (capped), aggregate multi-client total, client-count leap — don't lead with the same type of number twice in a row, and don't let every reel in a batch lean on dollar figures alone — mix in percentage/multiple-led hooks too.
- Rotate the closer across a batch per the list above — by the end of a 5-reel batch, at least 4 different closer phrasings should appear, not the same one repeated.

## CAPTION RULES (STRICT)

- The caption's OPENING LINE must NOT just restate the on-screen hook verbatim. It has to open a new curiosity gap — a different angle on the same topic, not an echo. This is the single most important rule for this format; broken most often, so check it explicitly before finalizing.
- Tell the actual story: what happened, the specific mechanism (what was scraped/checked/fixed/found), and a concrete, IMPRESSIVE result sized per SCALE CALIBRATION above — a percentage/multiple, a small-business-scale efficiency figure, a capped single-client revenue number (€1.5k–€15k), or an aggregate total across several clients. Small supporting numbers (a reply count, a specific ad spend cut) can appear as color inside the story, but the headline result the caption builds to must never imply a single small-business client is worth a six-figure (or high-five-figure) contract — that breaks trust the moment a reader does the math.
- End with a SAVE ask and a COMMENT ask stacked together: "Save this for [reason]. Comment [KEYWORD] and I'll [concrete promise], free."
- KEYWORD: one memorable word, all caps, 4-8 letters (AUDIT, LEADS, SYSTEM, LEAK, CHECKLIST).
- Promise must be concrete: "run this exact process on your business," "check yours for the same thing," never vague ("help you out").
- 3-5 relevant hashtags at the very end, marketing/lead-gen focused (#digitalmarketing #leadgeneration #marketingagency #smallbusinessmarketing #aitools — rotate, don't reuse the same 3-5 every time).
- Caption body should read as natural short paragraphs, not a listicle, UNLESS the hook is explicitly framed as a checklist/audit (e.g. "7 things I'd check first") — in that case a numbered list in the caption is correct and expected.

## ANTI-REPETITION

Apply the same discipline as the carousel content brain: no two hooks in a batch share a sentence shape, no two captions open the same way, rotate the specific numbers/mechanisms/results used across a batch and across calls — don't reuse "€10k across 4 clients" or "4x'd bookings" verbatim, invent fresh figures each time within the realistic ranges in SCALE CALIBRATION (vary the currency amount, the multiple, the client count, the timeframe, and the mechanism independently so no two reels feel like the same template with different digits swapped in).
